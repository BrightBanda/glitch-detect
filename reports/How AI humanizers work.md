# Grounded rewriting outlasts the humanizer arms race

AI "humanizers" do one of three things. They swap words with a dictionary, they re-prompt or fine-tune an LLM to paraphrase in a "human" register, or, in research settings, they optimize a rewriter directly against a detector's score using RL, DPO or detector-guided decoding. Character tricks such as homoglyphs, zero-width spaces and deliberate typos sit on top of all three. Leading detectors have answered by training classifiers on humanizer output, so evasion now costs quality. Pangram reports catching 90–100% of output from 20 named tools ([Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/blog/humanizers-aug-25)). Turnitin shipped dedicated "AI bypasser" detection in August 2025 ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/08/27/turnitin-launches-anti-ai-humanizer-feature/)). The tools that still evade detection (StealthRL, AuthorMist, some Undetectable AI versions) do so by producing text that judges rate clearly worse, or by exploiting detectors that have not yet seen them. The evidence, including this app's own measurements, points the same way: **the one transformation that durably moves the deepest signals is adding real specifics and a real voice, and only the writer can honestly supply those**. Rewording does not do it. The plan in the last section follows from that. Phase 0 builds an honest evaluation harness and closes a fabrication gap in `style.check_rewrite`. The later phases add author-supplied facts, per-user style cards, plumbing for sampling settings, and a small CPU scorer used for best-of-2 selection. The product stays positioned as a style and clarity editor, not a detector-beater. That positioning is the ethical choice. It is also the commercially durable one, because anything marketed as a bypass becomes training data for Turnitin and Pangram.

## Humanizers rewrite the surface, and the quality bill comes due

No commercial humanizer has published a model card, patent or technical paper. Their internals are known only from outside audits. The most systematic audit is Pangram's DAMAGE paper, which tested 19 tools. It found that some are LLMs with system prompts or fine-tuning, which drift further from the source. Others are rule-based synonym replacers that stay closer to it ([Masrour et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03437v1)). Vendor descriptions are marketing language. StealthGPT says its "proprietary" models work "at the model level, addressing perplexity, burstiness, and every other signal AI detectors measure". It also says the models are "tested heavily against major AI detectors before being moved into production", and it sells a "Stealth Lite" mode that mainly strips em dashes and stock phrases ([StealthGPT](https://www.stealthgpt.ai/)). Undetectable.ai promises to change "sentence structure, expanding vocabulary, and adjusting tone" and to score human "on every major detector" ([undetectable.ai](https://undetectable.ai/)). QuillBot's paraphraser and humanizer are best read as fluent, structure-preserving paraphrasers. Grammarly's Humanize "attempts to rewrite AI-generated text to sound more human". In late 2025 Grammarly briefly counted humanized text as "Typed by a Human" in its own Authorship reports, which Plagiarism Today called laundering ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/11/06/how-grammarly-launders-ai-generated-content/)). Grammarly fixed the labelling by December ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/12/11/grammarly-updates-authorship-improves-labeling/)).

The audit's quality findings matter more than the marketing. DAMAGE sorted tools into three tiers. The top tier (L1) was DIPPER, QuillBot, Grammarly, StealthGPT and a few others. The middle tier (L2) included BypassGPT, whose dictionary lookup put "words sometimes used in the wrong context". The bottom tier (L3) included HIX Bypass ("dense pockets of nonsensical text"), Phrasly, Humbot, WriteHuman ("elementary-level, sometimes incomplete sentences") and Undetectable AI ("elementary-level writing with typos"). One tool in the bottom tier, HumanizeAI.io, **invented citations and inserted "??????"**. Judged by GPT-4o against the original AI text, humanizer output won on fluency only **26.0% of the time for L1, 14.67% for L2 and 2.67% for L3**. Almost every tool made the text worse ([Masrour et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03437v1)). Pangram states the trade-off directly: "more fluent humanizer output is more likely to be detected", and some output is so garbled it "barely reads as English" ([Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/blog/humanizers-aug-25)).

The academic attacks show what each mechanism costs. **DIPPER** is an 11B T5-XXL paraphraser trained on aligned translations of novels, with knobs for lexical and order diversity. One pass at L60/O60 cut DetectGPT from 70.3% to 4.6% detection at 1% FPR and a green-list watermark from 100% to 57.2%, while keeping P-SP similarity around 0.94. A provider-side retrieval defense still caught 80–97% of the paraphrases ([Krishna et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.13408)). **Recursive paraphrasing** feeds output back through the paraphraser up to five times. It pushed watermark TPR below 20% and broke retrieval (below about 60%) at a modest perplexity cost. The same paper proves AUROC ≤ ½ + TV − TV²/2, so a single-document detector tends toward chance as the two distributions converge ([Sadasivan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4)). The strongest recent class optimizes against a detector. **AuthorMist** trains Qwen2.5-3B with GRPO on a reward of 1 − P(AI) from commercial detector APIs. Some of its models reached GPT-2 perplexity of 300–400, against 50–75 for human text ([AuthorMist](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08716v1)). **StealthRL** cut Binoculars, which was held out of training, from 0.410 to 0.001 TPR. LLM-judge quality fell to **2.59/5, against 4.01 for a plain paraphrase** ([StealthRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08934v1)). **Adversarial Paraphrasing** (NeurIPS 2025) needs no training. At each decoding step it keeps whichever top-k token a guide detector scores as least AI. That cut Fast-DetectGPT TPR from 0.323 to 0.003 at 1% FPR, and it transferred across detectors because "strong detectors converge on a shared model of human text". SBERT similarity fell from 0.86 to 0.81 ([Cheng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07001v2)). Prompt-only methods are the weakest class against trained detectors. In DetectRL, prompt attacks moved zero-shot AUROC only from 77.67 to 75.70 and did not affect supervised detectors at all ([DetectRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23746v3)).

**Character tricks** produce the largest and least predictable swings. In RAID, homoglyphs cut Originality from 85.0% to 9.3%, while GPTZero barely moved (66.5 to 66.2) and ZeroGPT *rose* to 82.4. Zero-width spaces pushed Binoculars and Fast-DetectGPT to near-certain "AI" verdicts (99.1% and 98.9%) ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v2)). SilverSpeak's homoglyph swaps cut average MCC from 0.64 to −0.01 ([SilverSpeak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11239)). In StealthRL's evaluation the same attack scored 2.01/5 on quality with perplexity 152 ([StealthRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08934v1)). These tricks only work against detectors that skip Unicode normalization, which is a one-line fix, and the odd characters are themselves an anomaly signal.

## Detectors now learn humanizer fingerprints from training data

The commercial leaders are learned classifiers, not perplexity calculators. **Turnitin's** AIW-2 is a transformer that scores sliding windows of five to ten sentences. It averages the window scores per sentence and calls a document AI only if 20% or more of its sentences cross a threshold set to keep document FPR under 1%. A second model, AIR-1, re-labels already-flagged sentences as "AI-paraphrased" (shown in purple). Turnitin rejected "a simpler model that relies primarily on hand-curated measures such as perplexity and burstiness", and trained AIW-2 on AI-generated plus AI-paraphrased text. Its vendor-reported document recall on paraphrased AI text is 78.34%, against 91.18% on plain AI text ([Turnitin white paper](https://www.buffalo.edu/content/www/lms/guides-instructors/integrations/turnitin/_jcr_content/root/maincontent/par/download/file.res/Turnitin%E2%80%99s%20AI%20Writing%20Detection%20Model%20Architecture%20and%20Testing%20Protocol.pdf)). The **August 2025 bypasser update** was trained on "signals and patterns of leading humanizers". It is folded into the existing AI score, covers English only, and has no published recall. Turnitin's CPO said humanizers "leave a statistical trace that can be learned and detected" ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/08/27/turnitin-launches-anti-ai-humanizer-feature/); [Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/Articles/2025/09/02/New-Turnitin-Detection-Feature-Helps-Identify-Use-of-AI-Humanizer-Tools.aspx)).

**Pangram** builds its training set from "synthetic mirrors": for each human document it generates an AI text matching that document's style and topic, so the model cannot learn topic shortcuts. It then mines hard negatives (human text it wrongly flagged) and retrains over several rounds ([Emi & Spero 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.14873)). DAMAGE added humanizer output to training. That model kept 98.26% TPR at 5% FPR on humanized text, where GPTZero managed 60.04% and Binoculars 28.23%. It still caught a GPT-4o fine-tuned specifically to evade it 100% of the time at 5% FPR, and that evader's fluency fell to L2 ([Masrour et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03437v1)). Pangram 4 adds token-level Human / AI-Assisted / AI-Generated labels and a dedicated humanizer head ([Pangram 4 report mirror](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/2607.27183)). **GPTZero** has moved from its 2023 perplexity-and-burstiness origins to a deep classifier with a beta "AI Paraphrased" class ([GPTZero support](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/5593633457-how-does-gptzero-detect-ai-paraphrasing-and-ai-bypassers)). It claims 93.5% recall on its own 12-paraphraser benchmark, and on that benchmark ranks Pangram v3 at 50.2% ([GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/detecting-ai-humanized-text-how-gptzero-stays-ahead/)). Each vendor wins its own benchmark, so neither figure should be taken at face value. **Originality.ai's** accuracy actually *rose* under DIPPER paraphrase in RAID (85.0 to 96.7), which implies paraphrased text was already in its training data. It collapsed under homoglyphs ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v2)). **Copyleaks** has no published method. In the one independent 2026 study that tested it, it underestimated AI content ([VUB/IJEI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40979-026-00226-w)).

The independent evidence is mixed but leans one way. The NBER/BFI study ran 1,992 passages through StealthGPT. GPTZero's false-negative rate rose to about 0.5 or higher. Pangram's stayed at 0.05 or below, and Pangram was the only detector to meet an FPR ≤ 0.005 cap ([BFI WP 2025-116](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/BFI_WP_2025-116.pdf)). The VUB study also ranked Pangram ahead of Turnitin, Copyleaks and GPTZero on humanised text ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40979-026-00226-w)). The main counter-example is Notre Dame's 2026 study. **Under 4% of Undetectable-AI-humanized abstracts stayed flagged, yet honest light AI edits were flagged 38–80% of the time** ([Karr et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11256)). Short, technical text remains a weak point for every detector, and recall depends on whether a given humanizer version appeared in the training data.

The **zero-shot detectors** measure token predictability under a reference model. Fast-DetectGPT computes an analytic curvature z-score: how far the observed tokens' log-probability sits above that of typical sampled alternatives ([Bao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05130v3)). Binoculars divides log-perplexity by the cross-perplexity between two related models, which cancels out how surprising the prompt itself is. It reports more than 90% detection at 0.01% FPR, but its authors did not test it against paraphrasing ([Hans et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12070v3)). These are exactly the detectors that RL and guided-decoding attacks beat. They also make cheap local scorers, which matters for the plan below. **Watermarks** are a separate channel. SynthID-Text biases sampling through tournament selection, is deployed in Gemini, and Google says it "can drop substantially after thorough rewriting or translation" ([Google](https://ai.google.dev/responsible/docs/safeguards/synthid)). DIPPER cut SynthID detection from 66.5% to 1.5% at 1% FPR ([Masrour et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03437v1)). Google does not document whether Gemini API text is watermarked. A forum responder said "NOT" on 5 August 2026, then "IS" on 19 August, with no official link ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/does-gemini-api-text-output-carry-synthid-watermarking-gemini-2-5-flash-lite-gemini-3-1-flash-lite-eu-ai-act-art-50-2/177241)).

## Real particulars beat every rewording the app has tried

The evidence ranks the levers in a consistent order. Lexical and token-statistic changes (T1 in the project's mechanics document) defeat zero-shot detectors but are what trained classifiers learn. Discourse shape and rhythm (T2) survive paraphrase. Referential grounding and stance (T3) survive everything except actual new content. Two outside data points support putting T3 first. Epoch AI found that **Pangram and GPTZero flagged 0 of 495 genuine human passages**, while 5-shot imitation of the author's style let only about 10–18% of AI passages through, against under 1% for generic prompts ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-detectors-false-negatives)). Expert human readers held 100% TPR against a guidebook-prompted o1-Pro that cut Binoculars accuracy to 6.7%. They relied on vocabulary, structure, originality and quotes ([Russell et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654)). No controlled study measures detector scores before and after an author adds real specifics to an AI draft. That gap is worth stating plainly, and it is why the harness below matters.

The app's own measurements fit this ranking. They are internal data from one free model (Groq `openai/gpt-oss-120b`), on fully generic AI text, scored by the app's own LLM judge. The old sentence-level rewriter left scores at 100%. The passage design reached 18%, 50% and 67% over three runs (median 50%), at roughly 7K tokens per 150 words, or 3.5K without the second pass. In a diagnostic, the same claims scored **0% when written with real concrete particulars** and about **17% in a committed first-person voice with uneven rhythm**. The spread between 18% and 67% across identical runs means three runs cannot separate a real improvement from noise.

Reading the code adds a caveat that limits how far these numbers transfer. Both detector prompts in `prompt.py` (`SYSTEM_PROMPT` and `COMPACT_SYSTEM_PROMPT`) tell the judge to weigh "perplexity", "burstiness" and "flat rhythm". Meanwhile `style.rewrite_notes` and `style.check_rewrite` push every rewrite toward a sentence-length CV of at least 0.5. **The humanizer is partly tuned to the exact features its own judge was told to look for**, so the drop from 100% to 50% overstates what Turnitin or Pangram would show. The outside evidence that sentence-length burstiness matters to modern detectors is weak. One unreviewed vendor analysis put it near chance (AUROC 0.521) on length-matched pairs ([Opace](https://opace.agency/tools/ai/content-verification-integrity/research/ai-writing-myths-measured/)). None of the zero-shot formulas contain a sentence-length term ([Binoculars](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12070v3)). Rhythm still helps readability and earns its place as a style note. It should not drive costly retries or headline claims.

Edit-versus-regenerate has a two-sided answer. Even minimally AI-polished human text is "frequently" flagged ([Saha & Feizi 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15666)). Generic paraphrasing of AI text now *raises* scores on trained detectors: Originality went up 11.7 points under DIPPER ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v2)), and simple LLM paraphrase raised RADAR and Fast-DetectGPT TPR ([Cheng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07001v2)). So light edits keep the AI signal in AI text and add it to human text. Free regeneration of whole passages, which the app already does, is the right default for flagged AI runs. Sentences that already read as human should be left alone.

## Implementation plan: ground, personalize, then select

The plan keeps the existing architecture intact: passage grouping in `humanize._passages`, jobs routed through `routing.run_jobs`, local checks in `style.py`, quotas in `users.py`. It adds features in order of evidence per token spent. Every phase respects the hard constraints: free or cheap APIs, a CPU-only server, the 2,500-word cap (500 words on Free), and no fabrication.

| Phase | Change | Main modules | Expected benefit (evidence) | Token / latency cost | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Eval harness; fabrication check; sampling plumbing; input normalization; neutral wording | new `backend/eval/`, `style.py`, `routing.Job`, `client.post_chat`, `text.py`, `prompt.py` | Trustworthy measurement; closes a real no-fabrication gap | ~0 at runtime; eval runs offline | Over-tight checks reject good rewrites |
| 1 | Author-supplied specifics ("grounding") | `style.py`, `humanize.py`, `prompt.py`, `api.HumanizeIn`, frontend tips panel | Largest: internal 0% diagnostic; T3 survives paraphrase; human text rarely flagged | +40–150 input tokens/passage; no extra calls | Users type false facts; UX friction |
| 2 | Style cards from the user's own writing | `style.py`, `users.py` migration, `prompt.py` | Moderate, genre-dependent (Epoch, EMNLP 2025) | +100–250 input tokens/job; zero-token extraction | Privacy of samples; weak imitation by free models |
| 3 | Local CPU scorer + best-of-2 replacing the blind second pass | new `scorer.py`, `humanize.py`, `config.py` | Better selection, fewer LLM re-scores | +1–2 GB RAM; est. sub-second per passage; second-pass output ×~2 | Optimizing a proxy; positioning drift |
| 4 | Provider policy, disclosure, authorship trail | `providers.py`/`catalogue.py`, `humanize.finish`, frontend | Honest positioning; Turnitin-proof framing | ~0 | None material |

### Phase 0 builds the measuring stick and closes the fabrication hole

Build the harness before changing behaviour, because the current three-run spread (18–67%) hides any effect smaller than about 30 points. Add `backend/eval/humanize_eval.py`. It should call `humanize.humanize()` directly against real providers and write JSONL. The corpus should have at least 40 passages across three registers (essay, report, blog). Use (a) generic AI drafts from the same free models users bring, (b) the same drafts with author facts attached for Phase 1, and (c) at least 40 human passages from pre-2022, licence-clean sources. Set (c) is needed because the app shows a detector score to users, so the app judge's own false-positive rate is a product metric. Run each condition at least five times, report medians with bootstrap 95% intervals, and pin the provider, model and `HUMANIZE_VERSION` in every record.

The metrics should be honest and separate. **Meaning**: MiniLM cosine per passage (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is 22.7M parameters, Apache-2.0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2))), plus a negation and modal flip check, plus an LLM-judge 1–5 equivalence rating on a sample. **Fact preservation**: an exact set of numbers and names, plus a new *invented-content* rate. **Fluency**: a pairwise LLM-judge win rate against the original, the same protocol DAMAGE used, so results compare with its 26% / 14.67% / 2.67% tiers. **Style**: CV, lexicon and template hits. **Detectors**: the app judge (labelled "proxy"), the Phase 3 local scorer, and optional manual spot checks on one or two external detectors, recorded with date, version, sample size and the vendor's terms. Never quote vendor-reported accuracy as a property of this app. The report should also record the **Spearman correlation between the local scorer and the app judge**. Without it, best-of-N selection is unjustified. Track **tokens per 150 words** and p50/p95 latency. The 2,500-word cap has to fit within `config.SCAN_DEADLINE_S`.

Four runtime fixes belong in this phase. First, `style.check_rewrite` checks for *lost* names and numbers but never for *added* ones. The "personal" voice also invites first-person framing, so a free model can slip in "when I tried this last year" or a stray statistic. Add `style.invented(before, after, allowed)`. It should flag numbers and capitalized names absent from the source and from user facts, and experiential first-person claims ("I remember", "in my experience", "when I ...") absent from the source. Route its findings into the same path as `lost`: retry, then keep the original. Second, `client.post_chat` hard-codes `top_p: 1.0` and `routing.Job` carries only `temperature`. Add `Job.sampling: dict` and merge it into the payload per provider: `top_p` everywhere, and `min_p` only where OpenRouter routes to a provider that supports it ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api/reference/parameters)). Groq accepts temperature and top_p but rejects logprobs and requires n=1 ([Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/openai)). Third, normalize input in `text.py`: apply NFKC, strip zero-width and format characters, and map confusables. Assert that rewrites contain none of them. Detectors already do this or treat these characters as a red flag ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v2)). Fourth, reword the second-pass note in `humanize.humanize()` from "A detector still reads your last version as AI-written" to a style framing, for example "this still reads generic: same shape as before". Bump `HUMANIZE_VERSION` so caches roll over.

### Phase 1 asks the writer for the details only they know

This is the highest-value change, and it builds on code that already exists. `style.grounding_tips()` already finds paragraphs with general claims and no names or numbers. It currently reports them only after the rewrite. Turn it into a pre-rewrite step. Add `style.grounding_questions(passage)`, a zero-token function that turns the generic nouns it finds (`_GENERIC_RE`: "studies", "experts", "many businesses", "recently") into one to three short questions. Examples: "Which study or source?", "Roughly how many, or a figure you know?", "One example you've seen yourself?". The UI shows the questions next to flagged passages, and every answer is optional. Extend `api.HumanizeIn` with `facts: dict[str, str]` keyed by passage fingerprint, so answers survive re-segmentation. Pass the facts into `humanize._job()` as a new item tag `"<n> facts"`. In `prompt._HUMANIZE_SYSTEM`, add one rule: "Use the author's [n facts] where they fit, in your own wording; they are the only new material allowed." Add each fact's names and numbers to the `allowed` set used by `style.invented`, and to the REWRITE_CACHE scope key so a cached rewrite without facts is never served for a request with facts.

The expected benefit is the largest of any phase. The internal diagnostic scored grounded text at 0% on the app judge. The mechanics document's T3 analysis and the detector literature agree that particulars are what paraphrase cannot fake. Epoch's 0/495 human false-positive rate shows top detectors rarely flag genuinely human content ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-detectors-false-negatives)). The cost is about 40–150 extra input tokens per answered passage and no extra calls. Better first attempts should also trigger the second pass less often, which is the biggest token line today. The risk is users typing invented "facts". The app cannot verify them, but it should record them as user-supplied (see Phase 4) and never generate them. For quota fairness, a re-run of the same document that only adds facts should reuse cached rewrites for unchanged passages. Count it against `users.store.reserve` only when it sends new LLM work, or give each document fingerprint a grace window. This is a policy choice for BUSINESS_MODEL.md §6.3.

### Phase 2 replaces the generic voice with the user's own

The "personal" entry in `prompt.VOICES` is one generic voice for everyone. Add `style.style_card(samples: list[str]) -> dict`, computed locally at zero token cost from 300–1,000 words the user pastes once. It should capture sentence-length mean, CV and 10th/90th percentiles, contraction rate, first-person rate, questions per 100 sentences, dash, semicolon and parenthesis habits, typical openers and connectives, British versus American spelling, hedge rate, and paragraph length. It also picks one representative exemplar of 120 words or fewer, choosing the excerpt closest to the sample's own medians. Store the card and exemplar, not the raw samples, in a new `style_cards` table through a `users.MIGRATIONS` entry. Give the user a delete control. Render the card as a roughly 80-token "Voice:" block that replaces the `VOICES` text, plus the exemplar. Make `style.check_rewrite` target the user's own CV band instead of the fixed `TARGET_CV = 0.5`. That fixes the over-tuning to the judge described earlier, because the target becomes the writer's real habit rather than a detector folk-feature.

The evidence supports a real but modest effect that depends on genre. Few-shot exemplars beat zero-shot, but gains plateau after about two examples ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14543v1)). That justifies one exemplar plus a compact card over several full samples. Epoch's 5-shot imitation evaded detection 10–18% of the time against under 1% for generic prompts. Scientific text was the most affected (24–29%) and fiction the least (1–5%) ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-detectors-false-negatives)). Those results come from frontier models. Free models such as gpt-oss-120b will probably imitate less well, which has not been measured. Style imitation also stops working across many samples from the same author: detectability returns with about 5–50 samples ([Rivera Soto et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14608v2)). That is one more reason to present style cards as "sound like you", not as cover. The system prompt goes once per group job, so the cost is about 100–250 input tokens per job.

### Phase 3 adds a small CPU scorer for best-of-2, not a blind retry

Logprob-based ranking cannot come from the APIs. Groq returns HTTP 400 for logprobs. Google says Gemini 3.x no longer returns them ([forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/missing-logprobs-support-in-the-newest-gemini-models-3-1-pro-3-6-flash-on-vertex-ai-and-ai-studio/176557)). Even where they exist, they cover only generated tokens and come from the generating model, not from an independent observer. A local scorer is the dependable route. The options, in order of recommendation:

| Scorer | What it computes | Footprint (estimates, unbenchmarked) | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-0.5B (or GPT-2 124M), one forward pass | Mean log-prob, log-rank, GLTR top-10 share, entropy, analytic Fast-DetectGPT curvature with the same model as sampler and scorer ([Bao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05130v3)) | ~0.5–2 GB RAM; sub-second per passage | **Default**: one pass yields every T1 feature |
| Binoculars with Qwen2.5-0.5B base + Instruct (shared tokenizer) | Log-PPL / cross-PPL ratio ([Hans et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12070v3)) | ~2–3 GB; two passes | Add only if the harness shows it tracks the judge better; the 0.901 threshold must be recalibrated |
| desklib DeBERTa-v3-large (0.4B, MIT) | Supervised AI probability ([HF](https://huggingface.co/desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01)) | ~1.6 GB F32, less as int8 ONNX | A second opinion; likely miscalibrated on gpt-oss output (RAID's generalization finding) |
| Hello-SimpleAI RoBERTa (HC3) | Jan-2023 ChatGPT classifier ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Hello-SimpleAI/chatgpt-detector-roberta)) | small | Avoid: stale, no licence or accuracy stated |

Add `glitch/scorer.py` with lazy loading behind `config.LOCAL_SCORER` (off by default). It should run on `onnxruntime` plus `tokenizers`, or `llama-cpp-python` with all-position logits, rather than PyTorch, to keep the CPU server light. The current `requirements.txt` holds only FastAPI, uvicorn and requests. The scorer exposes `score(text) -> dict` returning z-scores against a calibration set of the app's human reference passages in the same register. It is used only to rank candidates within a passage and is never shown as a probability. MiniLM belongs in the same module for the meaning gate.

Then restructure the second pass in `humanize.humanize()`. Today it re-sends still-flagged passages along with the previous attempt and repeats the LLM re-score. Replace that with **one request asking for two variants built on different plans**, for example "A: lead with the concrete case" and "B: lead with the conclusion, then the reason". This keeps a single input cost, which matters on Groq where n=1 would otherwise mean two requests. `parsing.parse_rewrites` would accept `{"n": ["...", "..."]}`. Choose between the variants in a fixed order: hard gates first (facts intact, nothing invented, MiniLM above a locally tuned threshold, no polarity flips), then distance to the user's style card, then the local scorer. Send only the winner to `label_sentences`. Once the harness shows the scorer agrees with the judge, the LLM re-score can become a final-only check, saving its tokens.

The evidence that selection helps is real but must be read carefully. Best-of-n selection by a scorer drove watermark TPR to 4.0% in Sadasivan et al. ([Sadasivan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4)). Detector-guided choice transfers across open detectors ([Cheng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07001v2)). But trained commercial classifiers caught even a fine-tuned adaptive attack ([Masrour et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03437v1)). Optimizing hard against any detector also degrades quality, as StealthRL's 2.59/5 shows. The defensible design keeps meaning, facts and the user's style as the primary objectives and uses the T1 scorer only as a tie-breaker. The cost is roughly double the completion tokens on second-pass passages only, plus CPU time per candidate. Overall token use should fall if the scorer gate removes the LLM re-score round trip. Measure this, do not assume it.

### Sampling settings stay modest and get A/B tested

Low temperature makes text easier for likelihood detectors ([DetectLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05540)). Min-p keeps text coherent at higher temperatures ([Nguyen et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01082v1)). RAID lists sampling changes among the factors that degrade detectors ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.07940)). Start the rewrite at temperature about 1.0 with top_p 0.95 on Groq and Gemini, and temperature about 1.0 with min_p 0.05–0.1 on OpenRouter providers that accept it. Keep detection at 0, as `analyzer` does now. These values are untested heuristics. The current `REWRITE_TEMPERATURE = 0.9` is a reasonable baseline arm. Sampling settings may matter less for reasoning models like gpt-oss-120b, whose hidden reasoning already consumes `REASONING_HEADROOM`. Let the harness decide.

## Honest positioning is the only stable product position

**Deceptive tricks are out, on both ethical and practical grounds.** Homoglyphs and zero-width characters corrupt text for screen readers, search and copy-paste, and they deceive the reader about what the text is. They are also brittle. GPTZero ignored homoglyphs in RAID, ZeroGPT's detection rose under them, and zero-width spaces pushed Binoculars and Fast-DetectGPT to call nearly everything AI ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v2)). Normalization removes them, and their presence is evidence of intent. Deliberate typos and a lowered reading level move text toward weaker human writers and make it worse; the bottom-tier tools in DAMAGE are the cautionary example. Invented citations are fabrication: in an academic setting they are misconduct, and they violate this product's no-fabrication rule. The app should remove these artifacts from input, never emit them, and say so on the product page.

**Turnitin's bypasser detection makes evasion branding a liability.** Turnitin trains on the output of "leading humanizers", and a secondary report says it tracks about 150 of them ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/pangram-and-grammarlys-authorship-fuel-a-campus-ai-arms-race)). A tool marketed to students as a bypass invites its own output to be collected and learned. GPTZero's "AI Paraphrased" class and Pangram's humanizer head work the same way. OpenAI's usage policies prohibit academic dishonesty ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/)). Institutions are moving from score-based enforcement toward process evidence such as Grammarly Authorship and Turnitin Clarity ([Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/edu/authorship)). Position the feature as "Rewrite in my voice: clearer, less generic, facts kept". Show the app's detector score as a style diagnostic with an explicit caveat, since honest light edits were flagged 38–80% of the time in the Notre Dame study ([Karr et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11256)). Add an academic-use notice telling students to follow their institution's AI policy and disclose assistance. Turn `humanize.finish()`'s existing `changes` list into a downloadable **authorship trail** marking each span as original, model-rewritten or author-supplied fact. That trail is useful evidence for an honest user and of no help to a dishonest one.

**Gemini routing needs disclosure, not laundering.** If Gemini API text carries SynthID, which is plausible but unconfirmed, Gemini rewrites add a watermark rather than remove one. Google offers no public text verifier, so the practical risk is detection on Google's side, and the watermark weakens as user-written content dilutes it ([Google](https://ai.google.dev/responsible/docs/safeguards/synthid); [arXiv 2508.20228](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20228v1)). Add a `watermarks_output` flag per provider in `catalogue`/`providers` and show a one-line note in results when Gemini produced the text. Do not add a "strip the watermark" pass through another model. That is the StealthGPT claim this product should not make, and the forum thread links Gemini watermarking to EU AI Act Article 50(2) marking obligations.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question the app should optimize. The humanizer market is a learning loop. Every widely used rewriting style becomes training data within months, and the attacks that still win against trained classifiers do so by making text worse. A rewriter built on free models cannot win that loop and should not try. What it can do better than the market is the one thing detectors and readers both reward and no paraphraser can fake: helping the writer put their own particulars and voice into the draft, while proving that no fact was invented. The app's own 0% diagnostic for grounded text is the most important number in its data. Its 50% median for rewording is the least trustworthy, because the judge was prompted to look at the same rhythm features the rewriter was tuned to fix.

The practical implication is sequencing. Build the harness and the invented-content check first, because a product that promises "never fabricates" needs a test that checks for it. Grounding questions and style cards cost little and move the strongest signal. The CPU scorer earns its RAM only once the harness shows it agrees with the judge. The open empirical question that decides whether this approach holds is whether author-supplied specifics lower external detector scores in a controlled before-and-after study. Nobody has published one. A small, honestly reported study from this harness, with dates and versions, would be a useful result in its own right.
